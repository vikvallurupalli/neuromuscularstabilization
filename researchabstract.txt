Static cervical load models approximate spinal compressive force as a function of flexion angle, but they do not directly quantify neuromuscular stabilization demands or account for dynamic motion. This experimental study tries  to find if dynamic kinematic parameters improve prediction of cervical muscle activation beyond static angle-based models. Using an inertial measurement unit (MPU6050) to capture cervical flexion angle (θ), angular velocity (ω), and derived angular acceleration (α), and surface electromyography (EMG) to measure upper trapezius activation, high-resolution repeated-measures data will be collected in controlled static and dynamic posture tasks.
EMG signals will be filtered, rectified, and normalized to maximum voluntary contraction (%MVC). Static torque proxies proportional to sin(θ) will be compared to dynamic models incorporating ω and α. We will perform linear, hierarchical, and segmented regression models evaluated using cross-validation, and bootstrap confidence intervals. Results will demonstrate that models incorporating dynamic motion parameters significantly reduce prediction error relative to static angle-only models. Additionally, nonlinear change-point analysis will identify a reproducible flexion threshold (θ*) associated with disproportionate increases in neuromuscular activation. These findings should suggest that dynamic stabilization demands will not be fully captured by static load assumptions and support the inclusion of kinematic velocity components in cervical biomechanical modeling frameworks. This case study provides a quantitative framework for evaluating dynamic neuromuscular compensation in cervical stabilization.
